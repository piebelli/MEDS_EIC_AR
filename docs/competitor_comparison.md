# MEDS-EIC-AR vs. other autoregressive EHR models

**Status:** first draft for discussion, 2026-11-20. **Owner:** Pietro. **Reviewer:** Matthew.

This is the competitor table from our call: the autoregressive (AR) EHR models we would want to show
parity with, whether their code is public, how each differs from MEDS-EIC-AR in tokenization,
architecture and filtering, why those differences could change performance, and what would have to
change in MEDS-EIC-AR to make it a copy of each.

Scope follows the call. Only AR generative models are compared. EveryQuery, supervised baselines and
encoder-style models are left out on purpose (§7).

## 1. MEDS-EIC-AR today, in brief

Everything in the table is stated relative to this. Read from `main` (commit 556e9a9); file pointers
are in §4.

- **Input:** any MEDS dataset. No table or modality allow-list.
- **Codes:** flat. One token per MEDS code string. No hierarchical split of ICD or ATC codes.
- **Numeric values:** 10 quantile bins per code, fused with the code into one token
    (`HR//value_[102.6,105.1)`). Raw value dropped.
- **Time:** one `TIMELINE//DELTA` token at every change of timestamp, binned on 22 fixed edges from
    1 minute to 40 years. `TIMELINE//START` and `TIMELINE//END` sentinels.
- **No age, time-of-day or calendar-year tokens** (`age: null`, `time_of_day: null`). Age is only
    recoverable from the delta after `MEDS_BIRTH`, only while birth is inside the context window, and
    only coarsely: the top delta bins are 20-40 years and over 40 years.
- **Static data omitted** (`static_inclusion_mode: OMIT`): sex, race and other null-time measurements
    never reach the model.
- **Filtering:** drop codes seen in fewer than 1,000 subjects (admission, discharge, birth and death
    codes exempt); drop subjects with fewer than 10 events.
- **Backbone:** Llama-style decoder (RoPE, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, no biases), 4 to 24 layers, default context
    512 tokens.
- **Generation:** ancestral sampling at temperature 1 with no top-k or top-p truncation, 20 trajectories
    per sample by default, Hugging Face or SGLang engine, rolling generation past the context length.

Three things in these tables differ in kind, not just in degree, from MEDS-EIC-AR and are the ones I would
expect to matter most. They are hypotheses to test, not findings.

1. **Demographics and age.** Every Tier 1 model gives the model sex, race and age. MEDS-EIC-AR never
    sees sex or race, and has at most a coarse age signal that is lost once birth leaves the context.
2. **Curated versus open vocabulary.** ETHOS and Curiosity model a few thousand hand-picked tokens.
    MEDS-EIC-AR models everything above a frequency threshold. This is the "easier task, fewer
    answerable questions" trade-off from the call.
3. **Context length.** 2,048 to 8,192 tokens for Tier 1, against a default of 512.

### 5.5 Context Clues and Foresight

Both reduce to tokenization switches. Context Clues is "no time tokens, long context, top-k vocabulary".
Foresight is "codes only, no values, no time, de-duplicated by day". Neither needs model changes when
the Llama backbone is kept. The Context Clues switch doubles as the cleanest test of whether the delta
tokens help.

Two 2026 tokenization papers are directly relevant to the knobs in §6 and should be cited: Guo et al.
(arXiv 2603.15644) report that fusing code and attribute into one token beats separate tokens on 73 of
74 tasks, which supports MEDS-EIC-AR's current value encoding against the ETHOS choice. Thukral et al.
(arXiv 2606.15447) report that encoding ICD hierarchy beats flat codes in a BERT-style model, which cuts
the other way.
